PROOF

Let

m = nq + r

where r = m mod n.

Then

r = m − nq.

Suppose d divides both m and n.

Since d divides m and d divides n,

d divides (m − nq).

Therefore, d divides r.

So every common divisor of m and n
is also a common divisor of n and r.

Now suppose d divides both n and r.

Since

m = nq + r,

d divides m.

Therefore, every common divisor of n and r
is also a common divisor of m and n.

Thus the pairs (m, n) and (n, r)
have exactly the same common divisors.

Since r = m mod n,

gcd(m, n) = gcd(n, m mod n).

Q.E.D.
