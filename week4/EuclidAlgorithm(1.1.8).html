Suppose m < n.

Then

m mod n = m.

Therefore, Euclid's algorithm changes the pair

(m, n) → (n, m).

After this step, the first number is greater than
or equal to the second number, and the algorithm
continues normally.

This can happen at most once during the execution,
because after the first step the numbers are in the
correct order.

Answer: It can happen at most once.
